# Research-gap chain

Existing knowledge → demonstrated limitation → scientific consequence → proposed contribution → falsifiable hypothesis → validating experiment.

1. *Known:* journal turnaround varies by field/journal/year and is dominated by within-journal article variation (Björk & Solomon 2013); publisher statistics are defined inconsistently and can deviate from article-level values (Bilalli 2021; Andersen 2021); turnaround drifts (Horbach 2020; Ellison 2002).
2. *Limitation:* none of these tests whether **past journal-level statistics predict future individual outcomes**, nor reports predictive intervals/probabilities with calibration.
3. *Consequence:* authors, funders and evaluators use "median days" or "fast journal" labels as if they were forecasts; if drift and within-journal dispersion are large, such labels give false precision, and "≤ T days" guarantees are unsupported.
4. *Contribution:* a protocolised out-of-time evaluation (visibility rule, cohorts, proper scores) over ~800 journals, with a ranking of simple forecasters and an explicit measure of how often "≤72 d" is supported.
5. *Hypotheses:* H1–H7 (`hypotheses.md`).
6. *Experiments:* E2–E7, validated by E8–E9.
