# R4 hostile-but-fair review: Methods_Manuscript.docx (cycle 3, Part D)

Scope: manuscript text vs `reinforced/results/D_*.json`, `D_panel_rows.csv`, `D_external_rows.csv`, `results/metrics.json`; protocol Part D + D-A1/D-A2; code `d_lib.py`, `d_experiments.py`, `d_external.py`, `d_sensitivity_cap.py`, `d_defaults.py`, `tdelay/protocol.py`. Own recomputations from the row-level CSVs are marked (R4-calc).

## 0. What checks out

- Every number in Tables 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and in the Results prose that I traced matches the source JSON to the printed precision. Examples: F0 -1.9 [-2.3,-1.5], abs 7.18; CV0 +0.5, 6.75; HD1 -0.42 [-0.54,-0.30], imprint t(6) [-0.70,-0.02], block [-0.57,-0.28], 6/7 imprints, 3/3 publishers; Holm p 2.9e-11/2.8e-07/8.0e-11 (Holm arithmetic verified); HD2 -0.14% [-0.15,-0.09]; interval score -0.007; HD5 r=0.15 [0.03,0.26], Spearman 0.20, slope 0.46 [0.09,0.94], pred -3.2 vs obs -1.9; tau2 0.041/0.038/0.041; cap table +2.2/-0.9/-2.0/-2.4 and 182-day agreement within 0.2; ICC 26.5% [24.7,28.3], 69.1%; 15.7%, -3.0, 77.0%; 12.0%/1.6% with CIs; external Table 4, HD6 (-0.50, -0.25 [-0.51,0.03], pinball -0.00025); cap-365 column of Table 5 (HD1 -0.37, HD2 -0.11%, CV0 +1.2 [0.8,1.6], r=-0.12).
- Noise floor reproduces (0.03618 normal approx; exact binomial 0.03621; "12%" = (3.558-3.135)/3.558 = 11.9%).
- Leakage in the fitting code: none found. `complete(o,t0)` = o+91+480 <= t0 is the right condition for a capped, received-date target; `learn()` is called only with `use=[o for o in origins if complete(o,t0)]`, and tau2, b, V's c, R's PIT map and ACI alpha all use only those origins' visible/target samples. `split_journal_origin` enforces V<t0, accepted<t0, received<t0 for the visible set and asserts target/visible disjointness. External transfer uses the frame parameters of the *matching* origin (`params[t0]`), so it is also out-of-time. (Residual caveats are in finding 11.)
- Disclosure of D-A2 is present in Methods, Table 5, Limitations, protocol, deviations log, and the commit order is consistent (D-A2 commit b4ff392 at 08:47, cap-480 results 4459a9b at 16:10).

The problems are therefore not arithmetic; they are claims that outrun the evidence, a confirmatory-status problem, and several design weaknesses that the manuscript does not surface.

## 1. Findings

### 1. MAJOR - "calibrated" (title, Abstract, Conclusions) and "small but consistent improvement" are not supported by the paper's own results
- Quoted: Title "a calibrated, strictly out-of-time method"; Abstract Conclusions "estimating it from earlier complete cohorts is a small but consistent improvement"; Conclusions "improves calibration modestly and consistently".
- Source values: (a) only one nominal level (80% interval) is ever checked for coverage; no PIT/quantile-level calibration is reported for CV0. (b) CV0 leaves 3.14 of F0's 3.56 points of "excess" absolute error (88%) (D_defaults_and_floor.json). (c) By publisher CV0 signed coverage is Springer +0.47, Wiley +0.09 but **Taylor & Francis +2.05** (D_summary.json `by_pub_signed_cov`), i.e. outside the HD3 band and further from 80% than F0 there (-0.71). By origin CV0 ranges -0.2 to +1.2. (d) Unit level (R4-calc, D_panel_rows.csv, 4,486 journal-origins): CV0 has smaller |cov-0.8| than F0 in 42.4%, ties in 19.9%, **larger in 37.7%**. The gain is a small shift in the mean, not a consistent per-journal improvement. (e) Interval score: imprint t(6) CI [-0.0136, +0.0012] crosses zero, only 2/3 publishers improve (T&F +0.0035); this is not in the manuscript, which quotes only the journal-cluster CI. (f) Externally CVg signed +1.1 [-0.1,+2.2], CVb +1.3 [+0.2,+2.4] (CI excludes 0); not different from V.
- Fix: drop "calibrated" from title and conclusions (use "recalibrated mean coverage at the 80% level"); replace "consistent" by "small average"; report the T&F over-coverage and the interval-score imprint CI in Results; state that HD3 was met in the pooled journal average but not within T&F.

### 2. MAJOR - Amendment D-A2: honest sequence, but the primary analysis is not confirmatory and the text overstates its independence
- Quoted: "Hypotheses were fixed beforehand; one design flaw (the duration cap) was corrected by a disclosed amendment"; "The choice of 480 days was made from the cap sensitivity of F0 alone"; Table 5 headline "Pre-specified hypotheses and outcomes in the primary analysis".
- Evidence: after the first run, HD5 had failed (r=-0.12, CI [-0.21,-0.02], i.e. significantly *wrong-signed*; slope -0.24 [-0.41,-0.04]) and CV0 over-covered (+1.2). The cap was changed, and at the new cap HD5 flips to r=+0.15 and HD3 passes cleanly. The cap candidates were {270,365,480,"none"} and the analyst knew from the sensitivity table the direction each cap pushes F0's shortfall (and hence CV0's over-correction). 480 was also "the cap used in cycle-2 Y1/Z2" on the *same frame and origins*, where strict-recalibration/cohort-variance results (Z2: R +1.9, V +1.1, F0 -1.9) were already known, so "before any CV0 result at cap 480" is literally true but the behaviour of the correction family at that cap was not unknown. The cap-365 run is labelled "as first specified" but the external test (HD6) was run only at the favourable cap ("not run at cap 365"). Further, CV0's form was developed from the cycle-2 drift/cohort-variance analyses on the same data realisation; only the external set is new data.
- Why it matters: a researcher-degree-of-freedom on a design factor that moves two of the pre-specified outcomes (HD3, HD5) cannot be repaired by disclosure; "supported" in Table 5 (primary column) reads as confirmatory.
- Fix: (i) call the cap-480 results "pre-specified hypotheses, post-hoc design (amended after seeing failures)"; (ii) say explicitly in Methods that HD5 failure and CV0 over-coverage preceded the amendment (currently only in Table 5/Limitations context); (iii) present cap 365 and 480 as co-primary in the abstract (HD5 fails at one, passes weakly at the other = "not established"); (iv) run HD6 at cap 365 too, or state it cannot be called confirmatory; (v) add a cap-robust evaluation (finding 7) that does not depend on choosing a cap.

### 3. MAJOR - HD5 "mechanism supported weakly" is not interpretable; sign depends on the cap and the association is largely explained by interval width
- Quoted: "We therefore regard the mechanism as supported weakly and conditionally on the cap"; Abstract "correlated weakly (r = 0.15)".
- Evidence: (a) The pre-specified criterion is only "correlates positively"; any r>0 passes. (b) r flips from -0.12 (cap 365, significantly negative) to +0.15 (cap 480) when only the cap changes; a mechanism test whose sign is governed by the scoring cap is dominated by the cap artefact the paper itself describes (journals with wide/slow upper tails are exactly those where the cap inflates observed coverage). (c) R4-calc on F0 rows: journal-level corr(pred, width)=0.47 and corr(obs, width)=0.13; within terciles of F0 interval width the obs/pred correlation falls to 0.10/0.11/0.06. So roughly half of r is the trivial fact that wide intervals are less sensitive to a location shift, not evidence for cohort-shift variance. (d) The predicted shift variance is a single publisher constant; between-journal variation in `pred_shortfall` comes only from s^2/n and sample shape, so the test cannot discriminate "cohort shift" from "visible-vs-target definition mismatch". Indeed the visible set is defined by first-online date and acceptance before t0 (right-truncated by acceptance) whereas the target is defined by received date; b_pub is positive (+0.01 to +0.05), as expected from truncation. Hence "cohort-to-cohort shift" in the Methods, title ("under cohort shift") and Discussion conflates real drift with a truncation/definition effect that is mechanical. (e) Protocol specified a decile-bin calibration slope; the code reports a journal-level OLS slope (0.46) - minor deviation.
- Fix: rename the construct ("visible-to-target shift, including truncation"); report HD5 with width (and n) partialled out and with journals whose q90 reaches the cap excluded, at both caps; downgrade the Abstract sentence to "the mechanism test was inconclusive (r=-0.12 at cap 365, +0.15 at cap 480)".

### 4. MAJOR - External validation: headline "close to the 1.9 points in the frame" is driven by five journals; the typical external journal is over-corrected
- Quoted: "F0 under-covered by 2.0 points ... close to the 1.9 points in the frame"; Abstract "CV0 reduced absolute coverage error against empirical quantiles (6.9 vs 7.4 points)".
- Evidence (D_external_summary.json `by_journal_f0_signed`, D_external_rows.csv; R4-calc): five Elsevier journals have F0 coverage 20-37 points below nominal (Int J Biol Macromol -37.3, J Hazard Mater -25.2, Food Chem -24.5, Biochem Pharmacol -22.2, Bioresour Technol -20.5); they alone contribute -1.1 of the -2.0 mean. Excluding them (112 journals): F0 signed -0.9, CVg signed **+2.0**, abs error F0 6.39 / CVg 6.08 / V 6.25, CVg-F0 -0.32, CVg-V -0.17. So the frozen correction over-covers in the typical external journal, and the "transfer" of the frame's average shortfall is a coincidence of mega-journal outliers (likely date/entry-date artefacts or fast-lane/special-issue regimes) that neither F0 nor CV0 handles. Median F0 journal error is -1.4. Nothing about this heterogeneity appears in the text.
- Further: PubMed entry date as "first online" differs by publisher practice (issue-based vs article-in-press entry), changes which papers are "visible" in the 12-month window and which are removed by the accepted-after-publication rule, and so directly alters the visible sample; no sensitivity to this is shown. The list is high-volume Elsevier-dominated and 10 of 127 journals dropped by the >=600-records / eligibility rules (selection on date availability). Table 4 "CV0 (frame tau^2, global)" is CVg, not CV0 as defined; the Abstract calls it CV0. "Frozen" is imprecise: parameters are re-learned for each origin from frame origins complete at that date (this is correct, but should be said). The secondary own-history analysis (CV0 abs 6.91 vs own-V 7.14) was run and is not reported.
- Fix: report the outlier journals, a trimmed/median analysis and a leave-the-five-out HD6; analyse by publisher; show an entry-date-vs-online-date sensitivity on a subset with both; relabel CVg; mention own-history results.

### 5. MODERATE - The "sampling-noise floor of 3.6" is not an attainable floor, so "excess error" and "removed 12%" are ill-defined
- Quoted: "The sampling-noise floor of the absolute error is 3.6 points"; "CV0 removed 12% of F0's excess error".
- Evidence: the floor (`d_defaults.py`) is E|Binomial(n_te,0.8)/n_te - 0.8| averaged over journals: it ignores (a) noise in the interval itself - the empirical q10-q90 interval from n_tr (median 281, min 100) papers has coverage s.d. about sqrt(0.18/n_tr) = 2.5 points on average; adding it to the binomial term in quadrature raises the floor to about 4.1 (R4-calc, independence approximation); (b) within-cohort dependence: Y3 reports 9.2% of records are batch acceptances and 49 fast-lane journals, producing overdispersion beyond binomial for any forecaster. The real attainable floor is therefore larger than 3.6 and "excess" is overstated; relative statements ("12% of excess") change materially.
- Fix: compute the floor by an exchangeable-null simulation that keeps the real training/target sizes and dependence (Y2 already has `cov_exchangeable` machinery): split each journal's pooled visible+target sample at random into pseudo-train/pseudo-target, apply F0 and CV0, and report |cov-0.8| there. Call 3.6 a lower bound.

### 6. MODERATE - Comparators: ACI is effectively F0, its explanation is wrong, F6 is quietly competitive, and no ablation shows the learned tau^2 matters
- Quoted: "ACI uses one global level that moved little because pooled coverage on the earlier origins was close to nominal (alpha stayed at 0.193)"; Abstract "beat ... adaptive updating"; "three standard corrections".
- Evidence: (a) alpha moves by gamma*(0.20-m) = 0.05 x (about 0.02-0.03) = about 0.001 per step for 1-6 steps; it stays near 0.2 because gamma is tiny and few steps exist, not because earlier coverage was near nominal (F0 under-covered by 2-3 points on those cohorts). The ACI as built (per-origin, journal-averaged m - the text says "pooled" but code averages per-journal miscoverage) is not Gibbs-Candes ACI in any operating sense. The HD4 "win over ACI" is therefore the HD1 win over F0 (identical 0.36 vs 0.42). (b) F6 (time-decayed, no parameters learned) has lower pinball (0.2019 vs CV0 0.2024) and lower interval score (2.398 vs 2.403) than CV0, and gets about half of CV0's absolute-error gain (-0.22 vs F0, imprint CI [-0.37,-0.007]); it was not made an HD comparator and is not discussed in the text. CVb also beats CV0 on pinball. External: F6 pinball 0.2101 < CV0 0.2105. (c) CVg (one global tau^2) = CV0 within 0.02 points (6.73 vs 6.75), and tau^2 hardly varies across origins (0.0382 with one earlier cohort to 0.0429 with six), so the "learned from earlier complete cohorts" machinery contributes almost nothing over a fixed constant; no ablation with tau=0 (h^2=s^2/n only) or a fixed constant 0.04 is shown. The Discussion claim that "the widening can be learned from the journal's peers using only information that is complete at the time" is untested against a constant. (d) V's grid step 0.025 produces coarse coverage granularity (c=1.075 gives +1.0).
- Fix: tune/describe ACI honestly (gamma on a per-observation scale, or drop ACI/rename "delayed level update"); add F6 and CV0-with-F6 to the comparison and state that F6 wins on proper scores; add ablations (tau=0, constant tau^2, finer V grid).

### 7. MODERATE - The cap-artefact claim is plausible but not isolated, "no cap" is not uncapped, and "+0.4 bias" has no interval
- Quoted: "A cap sets slow papers to the cap value, which lies inside the interval of every journal whose upper quantile reaches it"; "we use a 480-day cap, where the bias relative to no cap is +0.4 points".
- Evidence: (a) `d_sensitivity_cap.py` clips both training and target at the same cap; capping the training sample also moves the upper quantile to the cap (q90 = cap whenever >10% of the training sample is capped). The two effects are not separated; no analysis shows the artefact vanishes for journals whose q90 < cap. (b) The "1460/none" cell uses origins 2023-07 to 2024-07 whose 91-day targets are received 2024-07 to 2024-10; with data ending 2026-10-20 their observable D is limited to about 2-3 years, not 1,460 days, so "none" is itself right-censored (slow papers missing makes coverage look better; the true uncapped bias is at least as large). (c) +0.4 = -1.99 vs -2.42 are point estimates; the paired CI is not given (each CI is about +-0.45). (d) The sensitivity origins (2023-07..2024-07) differ from the primary six (2023-10..2025-01). (e) Statement is "evaluations ... can overstate calibration" generalised from one data set.
- Fix: score without the artefact: treat target D>=cap as censored and count a hit only when hi<cap or use journals with q90<cap/2; or evaluate each cap on journal-origins with identical sets; give the paired CI; relabel "none" as "1,460 / censored at data end".

### 8. MODERATE - Inference: independence assumptions are weaker than described
- (a) t(6) over seven "imprints" treats imprints as independent exchangeable clusters, but they are nested in three publishers (Springer/BMC/Nature all Springer Nature; Wiley/Blackwell) and one cluster is a residual "other" bucket (26 journals) alongside "Springer" (316). With publisher-level shocks the effective clusters are 3 (3/3 sign agreement has two-sided p=0.25). The lower HD1 limit -0.02 (cap 365: -0.0003) is practically zero. (b) Holm is applied only to the journal-level p-values (computed by a normal approximation to the bootstrap CI, hence values like 8.0e-11); the "more conservative" imprint intervals are not Holm-adjusted (ACI lower end -0.58 to -0.07 before adjustment). (c) Moving-block bootstrap with 6 origins and block length 2 gives about 5 distinct blocks; its CI [-0.57,-0.28] is not a credible origin-uncertainty statement. (d) All journals share the same six calendar origins, so common time shocks are not captured by the journal bootstrap.
- Fix: report publisher-level and imprint-level as descriptive; apply Holm to imprint CIs; drop or caveat the block bootstrap; do not print p-values below about 1e-4.

### 9. MODERATE - Event-probability results ("Brier", "slope") are mis-described and untested
- Quoted: Table 3 "Brier score and calibration slope ... of event probabilities"; "Empirical probabilities ... were too extreme (slope 0.89, 0.91 and 0.90)".
- Evidence (`d_lib.py`, `d_experiments.py`): the quantity is mean[(p - o)^2] with o = the *observed share* of the target cohort with D<=T, not a Brier score of binary outcomes (hence 0.009, an order of magnitude below typical Brier values); the slope is the OLS slope of observed share on p. (a) Slope < 1 arises mechanically from sampling error in p (errors-in-variables) and from any shrinkage; CV0 smooths p towards 0.5, so slope moving towards 1 is expected under a pure smoothing story and says nothing specific about cohort shift; the iid-null value of this slope is not given. (b) No CIs or tests for any Brier/slope difference; "CV0 lowered the Brier scores" is untested. (c) Text says CVb has "the lowest Brier scores" and "did so further for the shorter horizons", but at 180 days CV0 is lower (0.01217 vs 0.01229) and slopes tie at 120 days.
- Fix: define the metric; compute true binary Brier with n_te weights; give cluster CIs and the iid-null slope; correct the CVb wording.

### 10. MODERATE - Mixed designs in the Abstract: 15.7% and -3.0 come from a different, non-strict analysis
- Quoted: "Journal history lowered forecast loss by 15.7% relative to publisher-pooled forecasts, but empirical 80% intervals covered 1.9 points less than nominal".
- Source: 15.7% and -3.0 are from `results/metrics.json` (two origins, 182-day targets, uncapped, F2 = publisher-pooled "deliberately weak baseline"); -1.9 is the strict six-origin, cap-480 analysis. F2 is not in Table 1. The Results prose gives -3.0 then -1.9 without reconciling them (the exploratory "no cap" cell is -2.4; the remaining 0.6 is unexplained). Reader will take the 15.7% as a result of the strict design.
- Fix: say in the Abstract that 15.7% is from the original two-origin benchmark; report F2 in Table 1 or in a strict-design sentence; explain -3.0 vs -1.9.

### 11. MINOR/MODERATE - "Strictly out-of-time" holds for fitted parameters, not for all design choices; small observability gaps
- Fitted parameters: clean (see Section 0). Not out-of-time: cap (finding 2), F6 half-life, ACI gamma, V grid, 12-month window, quarterly origins, inherited from cycles examining the same 2022-2026 data; the frame was selected on 2023 article counts and 2023 date completeness (requires journals to keep reporting dates after the first learning origins); journal-origin eligibility (>=30 target papers) is conditioned on future volume.
- Completeness is defined by acceptance date, not by observability: papers accepted before t0 but first online after t0 (median accept-to-online 20 days, Y2) were not public at t0 yet enter the learned targets; the last complete origin's slowest papers are affected. Effect probably negligible but the wording "outcomes were complete at the origin" is slightly stronger than the code. Dataset end gives only 87 days of buffer for the final origin (2025-01-01 + 571 d = 2026-07-25 vs 2026-10-20).
- Fix: add a one-sentence limitation; optionally add an accept-to-online buffer to `complete()` as a robustness run.

### 12. MINOR - Journal-averaged vs paper-level statements
- Quoted: "covered 1.9 points less than nominal" (Abstract); "CV0 moved coverage to +0.5". Source: these are journal-averaged. Paper-weighted pooled signed coverage (R4-calc): F0 -2.7, CV0 -0.2, V +0.2, R +0.9. State which average is meant in the Abstract; HD3 in the protocol says "pooled".

### 13. MINOR - Abstract/Results wording errors
- "beat width inflation, recalibration and adaptive updating (imprint-level intervals just clear zero)": the imprint CIs for those three are [-0.45,-0.12], [-0.47,-0.13], [-0.58,-0.07]; the interval that "just clears zero" is HD1 vs F0 ([-0.70,-0.02]). Misplaced parenthesis.
- "the shares fell to 9-12% after excluding fast-lane journals, batch acceptances and low-reference articles": excluding batch records alone raises it to 12.5% (Y3_type_robustness.json); only the fast-lane/refs exclusions lower it (9.5-11%).
- "Pinball ... lower by 0.0003 ln-days": ln-units, not days.
- ACI "pooled miscoverage" is journal-averaged in code (`np.mean(v)` over journals).
- Software: shipped tau^2 is one global 0.040, whereas the evaluated CV0 uses publisher-specific tau^2; "runs the same formulas" should say so.
- Stale docstring in `d_experiments.py` ("nine evaluation origins 2023-07-01..2025-07-01"); the primary run needs default `D_CAP` (480) and reproduces cap 365 only with `D_CAP=365`; say so in the repository README.

## 2. Verdict

The numerical reporting is accurate: I found no mismatch between the manuscript and the result files, and the learning code respects the stated completeness rule, so the headline measurement result (empirical journal-history intervals under-cover by about 2 points when slow papers are handled) is credible and the cap-artefact warning is a useful, if not fully isolated, contribution. What does not hold up is the framing around the proposed method. The title's "calibrated" and the Abstract's "consistent improvement" are contradicted by T&F over-coverage (+2.05), the imprint-level interval for HD1 grazing zero, a 42% vs 38% per-unit win rate, an interval-score CI that crosses zero, and an external result that is neither better than width inflation nor representative once five outlying journals are removed (typical external journal: CV0 +2.0 over-covers). The gain over comparators is about 0.3 percentage points of an error metric whose "floor" is not attainable, against an ACI that is effectively F0, and F6 (a no-learning comparator) beats CV0 on pinball and interval score. HD5, the only mechanism test, flips sign with the cap, is significantly wrong-signed at the first specification, and is half-explained by interval width, so the "cohort-shift" mechanism is not established and is conflated with truncation/definition mismatch. D-A2 is disclosed honestly, but because the amendment followed an HD5 failure and a CV0 over-coverage and the new cap flipped both, the primary analysis should be presented as exploratory-after-amendment, with both caps co-primary and a cap-independent evaluation. With those reframings and the extra analyses listed (width-adjusted HD5, exchangeable-null floor, ablations, outlier-robust external analysis) the paper would be a defensible, modest measurement-plus-recalibration contribution; as written the claims of calibration, consistency and mechanism are overstated. Recommended decision: major revision.
